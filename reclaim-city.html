the premise

It sounds like a privacy lecture. It plays like a heist.

Your data is the most valuable thing in the city. It's just not yours. Reclaim City is about taking it back — district by district, server by server — while the city's surveillance keeps you one step behind.

how it plays

Thirteen districts. One map. All of it occupied.

You start where everything connects: your email. The Master Keys district. Everything else in the city builds on it.

01 / scout

Every district is a server.

Capitol, Marketplace, Archives, Vault, Perimeter — each of the thirteen districts runs its own missions, its own integrity, its own share of your data. You scout before you move, and you move where it counts.

The map is live. Districts are occupied until you clear their missions, and the city watches which way you go.

SECTOR MAP // LIVE
02 / the loop

Shut down the surveillance.

Data-privacy missions are the unit of play: trace a leak, scrub an archive, take a watcher offline. Each one nudges a district's integrity back toward yours and your exposure down toward zero.

Three numbers run the whole game — integrity, exposure, and streak — and every mission moves at least one of them.

03 / the promise

The game that practices what it preaches.

A privacy game should not need your data to play. Reclaim City runs entirely in your browser. No account. No data collected. Obviously. The whole pitch is the point: a city you can reclaim without surrendering yourself to reclaim it.
