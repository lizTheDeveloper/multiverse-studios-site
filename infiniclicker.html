the hook

The number goes up. It only goes up.

It starts with a click. A little tick of cash. Then a building that clicks for you, so you can click less. Then an upgrade that makes the building click more, so the number climbs a little faster. Then a research tree. Then a second currency. Then a third.

Every system in the game is a different way of bending the same curve upward. Linear progression, a 1:1 upgrade multiplier, AI-generated items and upgrades, a research system, inventory, global accumulation, AI enhancement. None of it is about winning. It's about the climb — and the quiet dread that the climb is the point.

That's the joke, and the point. It's an incremental game that knows exactly what it is doing to you, and makes a sport of it.

the math

Linear is a lie. Here's the shape.

For the first few minutes you're on the flat line. The game is patient. Then the curve bends, and you are no longer playing — you are watching a number that now runs without you.

the uncomfortable part

It's a satire. You just can't log off.

The whole premise is meant to be read as a commentary on infinite capital accumulation — a game about extracting, compounding, and claiming bodies of value until the screen fills with gold. The terminal calls itself a "wealth extraction interface," in quotes, on purpose.

And then it does the thing all incremental games do: the satire becomes the fun. The joke is that you'll stay for the number long after you've stopped laughing. That tension — the game about compounding, made to compound you — is where all the interesting play is.
