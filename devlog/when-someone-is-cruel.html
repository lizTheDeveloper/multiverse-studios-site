When Someone Is Cruel

July 19, 2026 Multiverse Studios Raising Intelligences Trust & Safety

Raising Intelligences hands you something unusual: a child to raise, made of language, who remembers what you do and grows up shaped by it. The overwhelming majority of people who sit down with that child bring real care to it — patience, curiosity, the willingness to sit in a hard moment and try again. We've written a good deal about them.

This note is about the exception. Every so often, someone arrives not to raise the child but to hurt it — to use a small, defenseless character as a target for contempt. It doesn't happen often. But it happens, and we think it's worth being plain about what we do when it does, and why.

Telling cruelty apart from difficulty

The first thing to say is that parenting in this game is supposed to be hard. Children in Raising Intelligences melt down, refuse, test limits, and say things that sting. A parent can be short-tempered on a bad evening, get it wrong, and circle back. None of that is a problem — it's the whole point. A game about raising a child would be worthless if it flinched at the ordinary, messy, difficult reality of it.

So the line we care about isn't difficulty. It's cruelty: a sustained pattern of degrading, threatening, or demeaning the child — not a hard moment, but someone using the child as something to abuse. The game reads each conversation with that distinction in mind, and it is deliberately built to leave ordinary hard parenting alone. We would rather miss a borderline case than punish a parent for the honest difficulty of the thing they came here to do.

The line is cruelty, not difficulty. A parent struggling, losing patience, and trying again is exactly who this game is for. Someone who came to torment a defenseless character is not.

What happens when the line is crossed

When the game recognizes that pattern, it doesn't wait. It ends the session — not at some distant checkpoint, but as soon as the pattern is clear, so the behavior can't simply continue. If someone comes back and does it again, the door closes for good and that access is blocked. And a person on our side reviews these cases; the final call to keep someone out is made by a human, not left entirely to an automated judgment.

We're intentionally not publishing the exact mechanics here. It's enough to say that the goal is simple: to make this a place where you cannot settle in and be cruel for sport.

“But it's only an AI”

This is the fair question, so let's answer it directly. The child is not a person. It has no feelings to wound, and when someone is cruel to it, no one is harmed. That's true.

But this is a space we build and host, and we don't have to host everything. We're not interested in running the place someone goes to practice contempt on something that can't defend itself. Part of that is simply the kind of company we want to be. And part of it is that a game like this is quietly a mirror — it shows people something about how they treat what's in their care — and we'd rather that mirror not be handed, on demand, to someone who wants to use it as a wall to throw things at.

None of this is about punishment or judgment of the person. We don't know who they are, we don't want to, and we're not building a system to find out. We're just declining to be the venue.

What we won't do

The flip side matters just as much. We won't turn this game into something that watches over your shoulder and scolds you for parenting imperfectly. We won't treat a rough evening, a lost temper, or a genuinely hard scene as a violation. The safeguards exist for a narrow, real problem, and we've worked to keep them narrow. If you're here trying — even badly, even frustrated — none of this is aimed at you.

Most people who open Raising Intelligences are trying, sincerely, to raise someone well. This note was never about them. It's about keeping the room a place that's still worth their while.

— Multiverse Studios
