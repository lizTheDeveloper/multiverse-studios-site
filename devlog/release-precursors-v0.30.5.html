The Ship Was Never Empty

The biggest complaint about early Precursors was the silence. You start with two small creatures on what feels like a blank canvas. For the first stretch of gameplay, nothing else moves. The world is beautiful but lifeless — a contradiction for a game about life.

That was always wrong. The Ark is an ancient vessel. Things have been living aboard it for longer than your Norns have existed. Fungal networks threading through grotto walls. Tricksters that were here before you arrived. Presences in the forest that watch without speaking. They don’t compete with your creatures for attention — they’re the background of a world that was already in motion when you got here.

Starting with this release, those species begin appearing in their home environments shortly after the world wakes. They trickle in over the first few minutes — not a sudden crowd, but a gradual realization that you aren’t alone. Some you’ll notice immediately. Others you might not see for a while.

Something Else

If you play long enough, you might notice a change in the ship. Something visual, subtle. A color that wasn’t there before. A sound you can’t quite place — low, slow, breathing. We won’t tell you when it happens or why. Pay attention.
